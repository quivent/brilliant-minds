# Elon Musk: Biographical Overview

## Early Life in South Africa (1971-1988)

Elon Reeve Musk was born on June 28, 1971, in Pretoria, South Africa. His mother, Maye Musk, was a Canadian-born model and dietitian, while his father, Errol Musk, was a South African electromechanical engineer. The family environment was complex; his parents divorced in 1981, after which Elon primarily lived with his father in various locations throughout South Africa.

Musk's intellectual gifts manifested early. He attended Waterkloof House Preparatory School before graduating from Pretoria Boys High School. At the age of 12, he taught himself computer programming and created a video game called *Blastar*, which he sold to a computer magazine for approximately $500. This early entrepreneurial act foreshadowed the trajectory of his career.

By his own accounts, Musk's childhood was difficult. He was an avid reader, reportedly reading encyclopedias when he ran out of books, and developed an intense interest in science fiction, particularly the works of Isaac Asimov and Douglas Adams. These influences would later shape his vision of humanity's future among the stars.

## Immigration and Education (1988-1995)

In 1988, at the age of 17, Musk left South Africa for Canada. He obtained Canadian citizenship through his mother's heritage, a strategic move that he later acknowledged was partly motivated by a desire to avoid mandatory military service in apartheid-era South Africa and partly because he saw Canada as a stepping stone to the United States.

Musk enrolled at Queen's University in Kingston, Ontario, in 1989. After two years, he transferred to the University of Pennsylvania, where he pursued a dual undergraduate degree. By 1997, he had earned a Bachelor of Science in Economics from the Wharton School and a Bachelor of Arts in Physics. This combination of business acumen and scientific understanding would become a hallmark of his approach to entrepreneurship.

Musk briefly enrolled in a PhD program in energy physics at Stanford University in 1995 but left after just two days to pursue business opportunities during the internet boom.

## Zip2: The First Venture (1995-1999)

In 1995, Musk co-founded Zip2 Corporation with his brother Kimbal Musk and business partner Greg Kouri. The company developed and marketed an internet city guide for the newspaper publishing industry, providing maps, directions, and yellow pages functionality for media companies.

The early days of Zip2 were marked by intense dedication and financial constraint. The Musk brothers lived in their small office, sleeping on futons and showering at a local YMCA to minimize expenses. Elon reportedly worked on coding while Kimbal focused on sales. Despite humble beginnings, Zip2 secured contracts with major newspapers including The New York Times and the Chicago Tribune.

In February 1999, Compaq Computer Corporation acquired Zip2 for $307 million in cash. Musk, holding a 7% stake in the company, received $22 million from the sale. At 27 years old, he had achieved his first major financial success.

## X.com and PayPal Era (1999-2002)

Rather than rest on his Zip2 windfall, Musk immediately reinvested a significant portion into his next venture. In March 1999, he co-founded X.com with Ed Ho, Harris Fricker, and Christopher Payne. The company was one of the first federally insured online banks in the United States, headquartered in Palo Alto, California.

X.com launched officially on December 7, 1999, with former Intuit CEO Bill Harris as the inaugural CEO. The platform's innovative approach to digital finance proved compelling; within two months of launch, X.com had attracted over 200,000 user signups.

In March 2000, X.com merged with Confinity, a competing financial services company founded by Max Levchin and Peter Thiel. Confinity had developed its own money transfer service called PayPal, which was proving more popular with users than X.com's traditional banking services. Within the merged entity, Musk served as CEO.

However, internal conflicts arose over the company's technical direction and strategic vision. In September 2000, while Musk was traveling in Australia for his honeymoon, the board voted to replace him as CEO with Peter Thiel. Musk remained the company's largest shareholder and continued on the board.

In June 2001, the company fully rebranded from X.com to PayPal. The following year, in October 2002, eBay acquired PayPal for $1.5 billion in stock. As the largest individual shareholder with 11.72% of the company, Musk received approximately $175.8 million from the transaction.

## Current Status (2025)

As of 2025, Elon Musk is the world's richest person, with an estimated net worth of $393 billion. His business empire spans electric vehicles (Tesla), space exploration (SpaceX), artificial intelligence (xAI), neurotechnology (Neuralink), infrastructure (The Boring Company), and social media (X, formerly Twitter).

In early 2025, Musk took on a significant role in the new Trump administration, serving as Senior Advisor to the President and leading the Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE). He departed this role on May 30, 2025, as planned when his 130-day special government employee term expired.

In November 2025, Tesla shareholders approved a landmark $1 trillion compensation package for Musk, one of the largest in corporate history, contingent on meeting ambitious financial and operational targets over the following decade.

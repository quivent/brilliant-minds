# Bjarne Stroustrup: Overview and Biography

## Personal Background

**Bjarne Stroustrup** (born December 30, 1950) is a Danish computer scientist who created the C++ programming language, one of the most influential and widely-used programming languages in history. His work has fundamentally shaped modern systems programming, embedded systems, game development, high-performance computing, and financial technology.

## Danish Roots and Early Education

Bjarne Stroustrup was born and raised in Aarhus, Denmark's second-largest city. Coming from a working-class family, he attended local schools including Laessoeesgade Skole and Marselisborg Gymnasium before pursuing higher education at Aarhus University.

From 1969 to 1975, Stroustrup studied at Aarhus University in the Department of Computer Science (then called DAIMI - Datalogisk Afdeling, Institut for Matematik). He graduated with a Candidatus Scientiarum (the Danish equivalent of a strong Master's degree) in mathematics with computer science, completing his degree with a thesis.

His early academic training in both mathematics and computer science provided the rigorous foundation that would later inform his language design philosophy - emphasizing both theoretical soundness and practical utility.

## Doctoral Work at Cambridge

Following his studies in Denmark, Stroustrup pursued doctoral research at the Computing Laboratory of Cambridge University in England. His Ph.D. work focused on the **design of distributed systems**, a topic that would prove instrumental in shaping his later contributions to programming language design.

At Cambridge, Stroustrup gained experience with the Simula programming language while conducting simulations for his distributed systems research. Simula, developed in Norway in the 1960s, was the first language to introduce object-oriented programming concepts including classes and inheritance. While Stroustrup appreciated Simula's elegant abstractions for program organization, he was frustrated by its poor runtime performance - a tension between abstraction and efficiency that would become central to his later work on C++.

Stroustrup received his Ph.D. in computer science from Cambridge and later became an honorary fellow of Churchill College, Cambridge.

## Bell Labs: The Birthplace of C++ (1979-2002)

In 1979, immediately after completing his Ph.D., Stroustrup joined AT&T Bell Laboratories in Murray Hill, New Jersey - one of the most prestigious industrial research laboratories in the world. Bell Labs had already produced numerous computing innovations, including the C programming language and the Unix operating system.

At Bell Labs, Stroustrup was given the freedom to "do something interesting." Drawing on his experiences with Simula at Cambridge and his appreciation for C's efficiency, he began developing **"C with Classes"** - the direct predecessor to C++. His goal was to create a language that combined C's performance and low-level hardware access with Simula's high-level abstractions for program organization.

### Leadership at Bell Labs

Stroustrup rose through the ranks at Bell Labs, eventually becoming the **head of the Large-scale Programming Research department** from its creation until late 2002. His research focused on distributed systems, operating systems, simulation, design, and programming.

Recognition of his contributions came steadily:
- **1993**: Named a Bell Labs Fellow
- **1996**: Named an AT&T Fellow

Following the 1984 breakup of the Bell System, Bell Labs became AT&T Bell Labs. After the 1995 AT&T breakup, the lab split into AT&T Labs and Lucent Technologies Bell Labs, with Stroustrup remaining a member of AT&T Labs - Research.

## Texas A&M University (2002-2014)

In 2002, Stroustrup transitioned to academia, becoming the **College of Engineering Chair Professor in Computer Science** at Texas A&M University. In 2011, he was elevated to the position of **University Distinguished Professor**.

At Texas A&M, Stroustrup was a key member of the **Parasol Lab** in the Computer Science and Engineering Department. He contributed to the development of **STAPL (Standard Template Adaptive Parallel Library)**, a framework for developing parallel programs in C++. He also founded the **Programming Techniques, Tools, and Languages Group** in 2003.

His academic work at Texas A&M focused on programming methodologies, language design, and developing tools for large-scale software development - themes consistent with his career-long interests.

## Morgan Stanley (2014-2022)

In January 2014, Stroustrup made a surprising move to the financial industry, joining **Morgan Stanley** in New York City as a **Managing Director** in the technology division. His role focused on the firm's data transfer infrastructure - ensuring fast, efficient, and secure movement of data across the organization.

Stroustrup has noted with characteristic humor that he was "the only managing director at the firm who does not manage a staff." In 2019, Morgan Stanley recognized his unique contributions by promoting him to become the firm's **first Technical Fellow** - a title created specifically for him.

He retired from Morgan Stanley on April 2, 2022, to dedicate more time to non-commercial C++ work, travel, and family.

## Columbia University (2022-Present)

Since July 2022, Stroustrup has served as a **Professor of Computer Science at Columbia University** in New York City. He had previously been a Visiting Professor at Columbia from 2014 to 2022, teaching an annual software design course using C++.

Columbia represents a return to full-time academic work, allowing Stroustrup to focus on teaching, research, and continued involvement with the C++ standards process.

## Additional Academic Appointments

Throughout his career, Stroustrup has maintained connections with multiple academic institutions:
- **Honorary Fellow**, Churchill College, Cambridge
- **Honorary Professor**, University of Aarhus (2010-2015)
- **Honorary Professor**, Xi'an Jiaotong University (2002-2006)
- Visiting positions at Cambridge, Columbia, and Princeton

## Recent Recognition (2025)

In 2025, Stroustrup received a distinguished honor in his native Denmark: inclusion in **Kraks Blaa Bog 2025** (the Danish equivalent of Britain's Who's Who). This publication features approximately 8,000 biographies of important and influential Danes - primarily royalty, industrialists, politicians, and artists. Stroustrup was among just 101 new entries for 2025, a rare honor for a computer scientist and testament to C++'s global impact.

## Personal Life

Stroustrup has been married to Marian Stroustrup, and they have two children. He has described his work-life balance as integrating his passion for programming language design with a commitment to family and personal interests.

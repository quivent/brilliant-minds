# John Carmack: Biographical Overview

## Personal Background

John D. Carmack II was born on August 21, 1970, in Shawnee Mission, Kansas. He is the son of Stan Carmack, a local television news reporter in the Kansas City metropolitan area. From an early age, Carmack displayed an intense fascination with computers and technology, marking him as what he later described as "the prototypical nerd" - a kid who kept to himself, played Dungeons and Dragons, and devoted himself entirely to understanding how computers worked.

Carmack attended both Shawnee Mission East High School in Prairie Village, Kansas, and Raytown South High School in nearby Raytown, Missouri. His teenage years were marked by an obsessive dedication to understanding computer systems, though not always through conventional means.

## The Juvenile Incident

At age 14, Carmack's determination to access computers led to a pivotal incident. He broke into a school with other teenagers to steal Apple II computers. To gain entry to the building, Carmack demonstrated his early technical ingenuity by creating a thermite mixture combined with Vaseline - a sticky substance capable of melting through windows. However, the plan went awry when an overweight accomplice struggled to fit through the hole, leading him to open the window instead and triggering a silent alarm that alerted police.

Carmack was arrested and sent for psychiatric evaluation. He was subsequently sentenced to a year in a juvenile home. This experience, rather than deterring him from computers, seemed to only intensify his focus on legitimate programming pursuits.

## Education and Early Career

After completing high school, Carmack enrolled at the University of Missouri-Kansas City. However, his approach to higher education was unconventional - he took only computer classes and showed little interest in general education requirements. After just two semesters, he dropped out to pursue programming full-time, confident that hands-on experience would serve him better than a formal degree.

Carmack was largely self-taught during the early days of computer gaming in the 1980s. He worked briefly as an independent contractor developing software for the Apple II platform, though this yielded very little income. His career trajectory changed when he landed a steady programming position at Softdisk, a computer company in Shreveport, Louisiana.

## The Birth of id Software

At Softdisk, Carmack worked on Softdisk G-S, an Apple IIGS publication. This position proved transformative, as it introduced him to John Romero, Tom Hall, and Adrian Carmack (no relation) - the team that would become the founding members of id Software.

Working together at Softdisk, the group created Commander Keen in their spare time. The first installment, "Commander Keen 1: Marooned on Mars," was released as shareware in 1990. The game was notable for its smooth side-scrolling graphics on PC hardware, which Carmack achieved through innovative programming techniques that mimicked the capabilities of Nintendo's consoles.

Following Commander Keen's success, the group made a bold decision. On February 1, 1991, John Carmack, John Romero, Tom Hall, and Adrian Carmack officially founded id Software, leaving Softdisk behind to forge their own path in the gaming industry.

## The id Software Era (1991-2013)

id Software quickly became one of the most influential game development studios in history, primarily due to Carmack's revolutionary technical innovations:

**Wolfenstein 3D (May 1992)**: This game popularized the first-person shooter genre, introducing players to a visceral, first-person perspective combat experience. Carmack's engine made real-time 3D graphics feasible on consumer PC hardware.

**Doom (December 10, 1993)**: Often considered one of the most important video games ever made, Doom refined and expanded upon Wolfenstein 3D's foundation. The game featured more complex level design, variable lighting, and networked multiplayer gaming. Doom became a cultural phenomenon and established the template for the entire FPS genre.

**Doom II: Hell on Earth (1994)** and **Final Doom (1996)**: These sequels built upon Doom's success while Carmack continued refining his engine technology.

**Quake (1996)**: Representing another quantum leap in technology, Quake featured fully 3D environments and characters rendered in real-time. It also pioneered internet multiplayer gaming, allowing players worldwide to compete in the same virtual spaces. Quake's engine became the foundation for countless other games.

**Quake II (1997)**, **Quake III: Arena (1999)**, **Doom 3 (2004)**, and **Quake 4 (2005)**: Each successive release demonstrated Carmack's relentless pursuit of graphical fidelity and technical excellence.

## Personal Life and Recognition

Carmack's success at id Software brought considerable wealth. By mid-1994, he had purchased two Ferraris: a Ferrari 328 and a Ferrari Testarossa. In a legendary moment of gaming history, he gave away the Ferrari 328 as the prize for "Red Annihilation," a 1997 Quake tournament won by Dennis "Thresh" Fong.

On January 1, 2000, Carmack married Katherine Anna Kang, who would go on to found and manage Armadillo Aerospace with him. The couple has children together.

On March 22, 2001, Carmack became the fourth person inducted into the Academy of Interactive Arts and Sciences' Hall of Fame, cementing his status as a gaming legend.

## Armadillo Aerospace (2000-2013)

Beyond gaming, Carmack harbored a passion for space exploration. In 2000, he invested his personal fortune into founding Armadillo Aerospace. He purchased a cavernous warehouse in Mesquite, Texas, and a 100-acre testing ground to pursue his dream of developing reusable rocket technology. Though the company eventually wound down operations, it represented Carmack's characteristic approach of diving deeply into new technical challenges.

## The Oculus VR Chapter (2013-2022)

On August 7, 2013, Carmack joined Oculus VR as Chief Technology Officer, drawn by the promise of virtual reality technology. On November 22, 2013, he formally resigned from id Software to work full-time at Oculus. His departure was reportedly precipitated by ZeniMax Media (id's parent company) refusing to support the Oculus Rift.

Upon joining, Oculus praised him effusively: "John is one of the brightest minds of our generation - pioneer, visionary, and industry legend. There are very few people in the world that can contribute to the Oculus Rift and the future of virtual reality like John can."

Carmack spearheaded Oculus's mobile efforts, focusing on making VR accessible and performant on portable hardware. However, he grew increasingly frustrated with Meta's corporate structure. On November 13, 2019, he stepped down from the full-time CTO role to become a "Consulting CTO," allowing him to pursue other interests while maintaining a connection to VR development.

In his departure memo from Meta in December 2022, Carmack expressed his frustrations candidly: "We have a ridiculous amount of people and resources, but we constantly self-sabotage and squander effort." He lamented that he had "never been able to kill stupid things before they cause damage, or set a direction and have a team actually stick to it."

Later, he clarified his aspirations: "I didn't want the Meta CTO job, I just wanted to be 'VR Dictator' where I knew the problem space intimately and was confident I could make a real difference if I had actual authority."

## Current Focus: Artificial General Intelligence

On August 19, 2022, Carmack announced he had raised $20 million for Keen Technologies, his new company focused on artificial general intelligence (AGI). On December 16, 2022, he officially left Oculus/Meta to dedicate himself fully to this new venture.

In September 2023, Carmack partnered with Richard S. Sutton, a renowned computer scientist from the Alberta Machine Intelligence Institute, to advance AI research efforts. This collaboration represents Carmack's latest technical obsession and potentially his most ambitious undertaking yet.

## Work Ethic

Throughout his career, Carmack has maintained an extraordinary work ethic. He claims to have worked sixty-hour weeks consistently - ten hours a day, six days a week - for the duration of his professional life. This dedication, combined with his exceptional technical abilities, has enabled him to make contributions that have fundamentally shaped multiple technology industries.

# Albert Einstein: A Biographical Overview

## Introduction

Albert Einstein (March 14, 1879 - April 18, 1955) stands as one of the most influential physicists in human history. His revolutionary theories fundamentally transformed our understanding of space, time, matter, and energy. Beyond his scientific achievements, Einstein became a cultural icon whose name became synonymous with genius itself.

## Early Life and Childhood (1879-1895)

Albert Einstein was born in Ulm, in the Kingdom of Wurttemberg in the German Empire, to Hermann Einstein and Pauline Koch. His father was a salesman and engineer, while his mother managed the household. The family was of secular Ashkenazi Jewish descent. Six weeks after Albert's birth, the family relocated to Munich, where his father and uncle Jakob founded Elektrotechnische Fabrik J. Einstein & Cie, a company that manufactured electrical equipment.

Einstein's only sibling, Maria "Maja" Einstein, was born in 1881. She would later earn a doctorate from the University of Bern in Romance languages and literature, demonstrating the intellectual atmosphere of the Einstein household.

Two pivotal moments shaped young Einstein's intellectual trajectory. At age five, while sick in bed, his father showed him a compass. The invisible forces that moved the needle sparked a lifelong fascination with electromagnetism and the hidden workings of nature. At age twelve, he discovered a geometry textbook that he called his "little book of wonders," igniting his passion for mathematics.

Einstein attended the Luitpold Gymnasium in Munich, where his independent and rebellious nature often clashed with the rigid, authoritarian educational methods of the time. His uncle Jakob nurtured his mathematical interests, introducing him to algebra and geometry outside of school.

## Education and Path to Physics (1895-1905)

In 1894, when Einstein was fifteen, his family moved to Milan, Italy, for business reasons. Einstein initially remained in Munich to complete his education but soon left the gymnasium and joined his family. In 1895, at sixteen, he attempted the entrance examination for the Swiss Federal Polytechnic School (later ETH Zurich) in Zurich. Though he excelled in physics and mathematics, he failed the general portion of the exam.

On the advice of the polytechnic's principal, Einstein completed his secondary education at the Argovian cantonal school in Aarau, Switzerland. During this time, he lodged with the Winteler family and fell in love with their daughter, Marie. In January 1896, Einstein renounced his German citizenship to avoid military conscription.

In October 1896, at just seventeen years old, Einstein enrolled at ETH Zurich as one of the youngest students. He studied physics and mathematics for four years, also taking courses in literature and history. His unconventional approach to education continued - he often skipped classes to study theoretical physics on his own, which led to conflicts with professors. In March 1899, he failed a practical physics course with the lowest possible grade after barely attending classes, preferring to study the masters of theoretical physics independently.

Einstein graduated in 1900 with a teaching diploma in mathematics and physics. However, he struggled to find academic employment. After working as a private tutor, he secured a position as a technical assistant (patent examiner) at the Swiss Patent Office in Bern in 1902.

## The Miracle Year and Rise to Prominence (1905-1915)

The year 1905 proved to be Einstein's *Annus Mirabilis* (miracle year). While working at the patent office, he published four groundbreaking papers that revolutionized physics:

1. **The Photoelectric Effect** - Proposed that light consists of discrete quanta (photons)
2. **Brownian Motion** - Provided empirical evidence for atomic theory
3. **Special Relativity** - Introduced the revolutionary concept that space and time are relative
4. **Mass-Energy Equivalence** - Derived the famous equation E = mc^2

That same year, he completed his doctoral dissertation at the University of Zurich. His academic career quickly ascended: Privatdozent in Bern (1908), Professor Extraordinary at the University of Zurich (1909), Professor of Theoretical Physics at Prague (1911), and return to ETH Zurich (1912).

In 1914, Einstein moved to Berlin to become a member of the Prussian Academy of Sciences and director of the Kaiser Wilhelm Institute for Physics. In November 1915, he completed his masterwork: the General Theory of Relativity.

## Personal Life and Family

Einstein's personal life was complex and often troubled. At ETH Zurich, he met Mileva Maric, the only woman in his physics class and a brilliant student in her own right. They married on January 6, 1903.

Before their marriage, Mileva gave birth to a daughter named Lieserl in January 1902 at her parents' home in Novi Sad, Serbia. Born out of wedlock, Lieserl's existence was kept secret. Her fate remains unknown - historians believe she may have died of scarlet fever in 1903 or been given up for adoption.

The couple had two sons:
- **Hans Albert Einstein** (1904-1973) - Born in Bern, he became a professor of hydraulic engineering at UC Berkeley, recognized for research on sediment transport
- **Eduard Einstein** (1910-1965) - Born in Zurich, he showed early promise but was diagnosed with schizophrenia in his twenties

Einstein's marriage to Mileva deteriorated as his fame grew. They divorced in 1919. That same year, he married his cousin Elsa Lowenthal, with whom he had been having an affair since 1912. Elsa's daughters from a previous marriage, Margot and Ilse, became his stepdaughters. Elsa died in 1936.

## The American Years (1933-1955)

With the rise of Nazism, Einstein, who was Jewish, faced increasing persecution in Germany. In 1933, while visiting the United States, the Nazis came to power. Einstein never returned to Germany, instead accepting a position at the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton, New Jersey. He became a U.S. citizen in 1940.

In 1939, Einstein signed the famous letter to President Franklin D. Roosevelt, drafted by physicist Leo Szilard, warning that Nazi Germany might develop atomic weapons and recommending that the United States begin its own nuclear research. This letter contributed to the establishment of the Manhattan Project, though Einstein himself did not participate in the weapons development.

After World War II, Einstein became an advocate for nuclear disarmament and world government. In 1952, he was offered the presidency of Israel following the death of Chaim Weizmann, but he declined, feeling he lacked the natural aptitude for dealing with people.

## Final Days and Legacy

Einstein continued working on his unified field theory until the end of his life, attempting to reconcile general relativity with electromagnetism - a quest that remained unfulfilled.

On April 17, 1955, Einstein experienced internal bleeding caused by a ruptured abdominal aortic aneurysm. He refused surgery, stating: "I want to go when I want. It is tasteless to prolong life artificially. I have done my share; it is time to go. I will do it elegantly."

One week before his death, Einstein signed his final letter - a letter to Bertrand Russell agreeing to add his name to a manifesto urging all nations to abandon nuclear weapons. He died at Princeton Hospital on April 18, 1955, at age 76.

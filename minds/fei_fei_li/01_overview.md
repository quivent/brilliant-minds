# Fei-Fei Li: Biographical Overview

## The "Godmother of AI"

Fei-Fei Li (born July 3, 1976) is a Chinese-born American computer scientist who has become one of the most influential figures in artificial intelligence. Often referred to as the "Godmother of AI," she is best known for creating ImageNet, the massive visual database that catalyzed the deep learning revolution and enabled rapid advances in computer vision throughout the 2010s. Her work serves as the foundation for many modern image-recognition AI systems.

## Early Life in China

Fei-Fei Li was born in Beijing, China, to Kuang Ying, a secondary-school teacher, and Li Sun, a computer department employee at a chemical factory. She was raised in Chengdu, in the Sichuan province of southwestern China. Her early years in China were marked by intellectual curiosity and academic dedication, traits that would define her remarkable career.

When Li was 12 years old, her father emigrated to the United States, seeking better opportunities for the family. In 1992, when Li was 16, she and her mother joined her father in America, driven by the desire to access better educational opportunities and to escape China's political discord at the time.

## The Immigrant Experience

The Li family's American journey began in Parsippany, New Jersey, where they lived in a modest one-bedroom apartment. The transition was challenging: the family arrived with less than $20 in their pockets and had to completely rebuild their lives. Her father worked as a camera repairman, while her mother took a job as a supermarket cashier.

During her high school years at Parsippany High School, Li worked part-time as a waitress and cleaner to help support her family financially. A pivotal figure in her adaptation to American life was her high school math teacher, Bob Sabella, who helped her navigate the challenges of learning a new language. Sabella and his wife went beyond their role as educators, lending money to Li's parents to help them purchase a dry-cleaning business. This act of kindness profoundly impacted the family's trajectory.

Throughout her undergraduate years at Princeton, Li would return home most weekends to work in her parents' dry-cleaning store, demonstrating her deep commitment to family even as she pursued academic excellence.

## Educational Journey

### Princeton University (1995-1999)

In 1995, Li received a full scholarship to Princeton University, a moment so unexpected that she asked two different high school advisors to review the acceptance letter. At Princeton, she chose to study physics, drawn to fundamental questions about the nature of the universe. She graduated in 1999 with a Bachelor of Arts degree in physics, earning High Honors for her academic performance.

### California Institute of Technology (1999-2005)

Li's doctoral work at Caltech marked a crucial pivot in her intellectual journey. While her initial focus had been on physics, her time at Caltech inspired new interests in computer vision and neuroscience. Her thesis, titled "Visual Recognition: Computational Models and Human Psychophysics," developed hierarchical Bayesian models to simulate human-like scene understanding.

Her doctoral research focused on computational models for visual recognition, emphasizing probabilistic approaches to object and scene categorization that integrated empirical data on human psychophysics. During this period, Li played a pivotal role in advancing "one-shot learning," a method applied in computer vision and natural language processing that enables AI systems to generate predictions with limited training data.

She received her Ph.D. in electrical engineering from Caltech in 2005. Both Princeton and Caltech have recognized her achievements: she was named Distinguished Alumni by Princeton in 2020 and by Caltech in 2024.

## Academic Career

### Early Faculty Positions (2005-2009)

After completing her doctorate, Li began her academic career as an assistant professor in the Electrical and Computer Engineering Department at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign (2005-2006). She then moved to Princeton University, serving as an assistant professor in the Computer Science Department from 2007 to 2009.

### Stanford University (2009-Present)

Li joined Stanford University in 2009 as an assistant professor in the Computer Science Department. Her rise at Stanford was remarkably rapid: she was promoted to Associate Professor with tenure in 2012, an unusually swift progression at a top-tier research institution. She became a full professor in 2018.

At Stanford, Li has held several distinguished positions:

- **Director of Stanford Artificial Intelligence Laboratory (SAIL)**: 2013-2018
- **Sequoia Capital Professor of Computer Science**: The inaugural holder of this endowed chair
- **Co-Director of Stanford Human-Centered AI Institute (HAI)**: Founding co-director
- **Co-Director of Stanford Vision and Learning Lab (SVL)**: Ongoing leadership role

### Google Cloud (2017-2018)

During a sabbatical from Stanford from January 2017 to September 2018, Li served as Vice President at Google and Chief Scientist of AI/ML at Google Cloud. This industry experience gave her valuable perspective on the practical applications and commercial potential of AI technology.

## Leadership and Advisory Roles

Li's influence extends well beyond Stanford:

- **Twitter Board of Directors**: Independent director from May 2020 until October 2022
- **United Nations Scientific Advisory Board**: Member since August 2023, appointed by Secretary-General Antonio Guterres
- **California Future of Work Commission**: Member for the Governor of California (2019-2020)
- **National AI Research Resource Task Force (NAIRR)**: Member for the White House OSTP and NSF (2021-2022)
- **World Labs**: Co-founder and CEO of this AI company focusing on spatial intelligence and generative AI

## Personal Life

Li is married to Silvio Savarese, also a Stanford professor and AI researcher. Together they have two children, a son and a daughter. Their shared passion for AI research has made their household a unique intersection of cutting-edge technology and family life.

## Recognition and Honors

Li's contributions have earned her numerous accolades:

- **TIME Person of the Year** (2025)
- **TIME 100 AI Most Influential People** (2023)
- **ACM Fellow** (2018) - for "contributions in building large knowledge bases for machine learning and visual understanding"
- **Queen Elizabeth Prize for Engineering** (2025) - jointly with Yoshua Bengio, Bill Dally, Geoffrey Hinton, John Hopfield, Jen-Hsun Huang, and Yann LeCun
- **Yale Honorary Degree** (2025)
- **Elected Member**: National Academy of Engineering (NAE), National Academy of Medicine (NAM), American Academy of Arts and Sciences (AAAS)

## The Immigrant Story as AI Philosophy

Li's journey from a teenage immigrant working in her parents' dry-cleaning store to becoming one of the most influential AI researchers in the world profoundly shapes her philosophy about technology. Her emphasis on human-centered AI, diversity in the field, and ensuring that AI benefits all of humanity can be traced directly to her own experiences navigating systems as an outsider and understanding the importance of opportunity and inclusion.

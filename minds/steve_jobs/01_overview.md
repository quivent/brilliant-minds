# Steve Jobs: A Biographical Overview

## Early Life and Education

Steven Paul Jobs was born on February 24, 1955, in San Francisco, California. His biological parents were Joanne Schieble, an American graduate student, and Abdulfattah Jandali, a Syrian-born political science professor. At the time of his birth, his parents were unmarried, and Jobs was given up for adoption just one week after his birth. He was adopted by Paul and Clara Jobs of Mountain View, California, who raised him in what would later become known as Silicon Valley.

Jobs graduated from Homestead High School in 1972 and enrolled at Reed College in Portland, Oregon. However, he dropped out after just one semester, as the expensive tuition was draining his parents' savings. Rather than leave entirely, Jobs audited classes for the next 18 months, famously attending a calligraphy course that would later influence the elegant typography of the Macintosh computer.

In 1973, Jobs took a job at Atari Inc., the pioneering video game company. The following year, in 1974, he embarked on a seven-month spiritual journey to India, seeking enlightenment. This trip exposed him to Buddhism and Zen philosophy, which would profoundly influence his design aesthetic and approach to simplicity throughout his career.

## The Founding of Apple Computer

On April 1, 1976, Steve Jobs, Steve Wozniak, and Ronald Wayne founded Apple Computer Company in the garage of Jobs' parents' home in Los Altos, California. The trio raised their initial capital of approximately $1,300 by selling Jobs' Volkswagen Microbus and Wozniak's Hewlett-Packard scientific calculator.

Jobs and Wozniak shared a revolutionary vision: making computers small enough and accessible enough for people to have them in their homes and offices. While Wozniak was the technical genius who designed the circuits, Jobs was the visionary who understood how to market and position these machines for the mass market.

The Apple I was essentially a hobbyist's circuit board, but the Apple II, introduced in 1977, revolutionized the computer industry. It was the first commercially successful personal computer to feature color graphics. Sales of Apple products skyrocketed from $7.8 million in 1978 to $117 million in 1980, the year Apple went public in one of the most successful IPOs in history.

In 1984, Apple introduced the Macintosh, unveiled during the famous "1984" Super Bowl commercial directed by Ridley Scott. The Macintosh was the first commercially successful personal computer to feature a graphical user interface, built-in screen, and mouse, making computers accessible to non-technical users.

## Departure from Apple

Despite his instrumental role in building Apple, Jobs' tenure at the company he co-founded came to an abrupt end in 1985. Following a prolonged power struggle with the company's board and its then-CEO John Sculley (whom Jobs himself had recruited from PepsiCo), Jobs was stripped of his managerial duties. He resigned from Apple in September 1985, taking several key employees with him.

## NeXT Computer

Shortly after leaving Apple, Jobs founded NeXT Inc. in 1985, a computer platform development company that specialized in powerful workstation computers for the higher education and business markets. His funding partners included Texas billionaire Ross Perot and Japanese electronics giant Canon Inc.

The NeXT computer was notable for its advanced engineering design and innovative operating system, NeXTSTEP, which was built on a Unix foundation and featured object-oriented programming capabilities. However, despite its technical sophistication, the NeXT computer was eclipsed in the marketplace by less costly alternatives from competitors like Sun Microsystems.

Though NeXT never achieved commercial success as a hardware company, its software legacy would prove invaluable. The NeXTSTEP operating system would eventually become the foundation for Mac OS X, and the WorldWideWeb (the first web browser) was developed by Tim Berners-Lee on a NeXT computer.

## Pixar Animation Studios

In 1986, Jobs purchased the computer graphics division of Lucasfilm for $10 million, spinning it off as an independent company called Pixar. Initially focused on selling high-end graphics hardware and software, the company struggled financially. Jobs personally invested approximately $50 million to keep Pixar afloat through 1991.

The company's fortunes changed dramatically with the release of "Toy Story" in 1995, the first entirely computer-animated feature film. Produced in partnership with Disney, "Toy Story" was both a critical and commercial triumph, revolutionizing the animation industry.

One week after "Toy Story's" theatrical release, Pixar went public. The IPO was spectacularly successful, and Jobs' stake in the company was suddenly worth $1.5 billion, more than his net worth had ever been during his first tenure at Apple.

Under Jobs' ownership, Pixar produced a string of critically acclaimed and commercially successful films, including "A Bug's Life" (1998), "Toy Story 2" (1999), "Monsters, Inc." (2001), "Finding Nemo" (2003), and "The Incredibles" (2004). In 2006, The Walt Disney Company acquired Pixar for $7.4 billion, making Jobs Disney's largest individual shareholder and earning him a seat on Disney's board of directors.

## Return to Apple

By 1996, Apple Computer was struggling. The company had lost significant market share and was searching for a modern operating system to replace its aging Mac OS. In December 1996, Apple announced it would acquire NeXT for $400 million, bringing Jobs back to the company he had co-founded two decades earlier.

Initially, Jobs served as an "informal adviser" to Apple CEO Gil Amelio. However, after the board ousted Amelio in July 1997, Jobs assumed the role of interim CEO, or "iCEO" as he called himself. He would not officially become permanent CEO until 2000.

Jobs immediately began restructuring the company, cutting the product line from approximately 350 items to just 10. He negotiated a crucial $150 million investment from Microsoft and launched the iconic "Think Different" advertising campaign, which repositioned Apple as the brand for creative rebels and visionaries.

The subsequent years saw an unprecedented string of successful product launches: the iMac (1998), iPod (2001), iTunes Music Store (2003), iPhone (2007), and iPad (2010). Under Jobs' leadership, Apple transformed from a struggling computer company into the world's most valuable publicly traded company.

## Illness and Death

In 2003, Jobs was diagnosed with a rare form of pancreatic cancer called a pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor. He initially resisted surgery, exploring alternative treatments, but eventually underwent a successful operation in 2004.

In 2009, Jobs received a liver transplant during a medical leave of absence. He returned to work but continued to face health challenges. On August 24, 2011, Jobs resigned as CEO of Apple, recommending Tim Cook as his successor. He remained chairman of the board.

Steve Jobs died on October 5, 2011, at the age of 56, due to complications from pancreatic cancer. He passed away at his home in Palo Alto, California, surrounded by his family.

## Personal Life

Jobs married Laurene Powell in 1991, and they had three children together: Reed, Erin, and Eve. Jobs also had a daughter, Lisa Brennan-Jobs, from a previous relationship with artist Chrisann Brennan.

Jobs was known for his intense personality, his pursuit of perfection, and his famous "reality distortion field," a term coined by colleagues to describe his ability to convince others that seemingly impossible goals were achievable. His legacy continues to influence technology, design, and business leadership to this day.
